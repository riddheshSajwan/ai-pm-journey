### **1. Watching session: P1 writes a post, the way she normally does**

**When:** Fri 25 Sep 2026, 11:02 am to 12:12 pm, Google Meet screen share.

**Who:** P1, content marketer at a ~300-person data and AI services firm. Owns the company LinkedIn page along with blogs and case studies.

**Ask:** "Write your next post exactly the way you normally would. I'll just watch." Topic was her Tuesday slot: why GenAI pilots in banks stall after the POC.

|**Time**|**What she did**|**What she said**|
|---|---|---|
|0:00|Opens a Google Doc called "LI drafts Sept". 11 half-finished drafts in it. Picks one from 14 Sep, two lines long.|"This is my graveyard."|
|0:03|Topic was already decided in their Monday content call.|"Topic is never the problem."|

|0:28|Starts the hook. Rewrites the first line 5 times (I counted).||

**After:** her manager replied Monday with a rewritten hook. The post went live Tue 29 Sep at 10 am. About four days from start to live, for roughly 180 words.

**What I saw that the interviews didn't tell me**

- Hands-on time was about 65 minutes, not half a day. The "half day" is mostly waiting: 38 minutes on the architect, then the whole weekend on the manager. In her head, all of it is "writing time".
- Research was the biggest single block: 19 minutes, most of it chasing one stat she ended up dropping.
- ChatGPT was used twice, only for polishing. The from-scratch draft was rejected in under a minute.
- A workaround nobody mentioned in interviews: the drafts graveyard. 11 posts started, none finished.
- Two people involved in one post that she never mentioned when describing her process: the architect and the manager.

### **3. What I changed, and what happened after**

**The one thing I changed:** I cut the draft. Brief only. P2's feedback, P3's feedback and the watching session all pointed the same way: the brief is the part people keep.

**First try of the change: second session with P1, Tue 29 Sep, 8:30 to 9:05 pm, screen share.**

Her Tuesday topic: what insurers get wrong about claims automation. I sent a brief-only pack at 6 pm.

|**Time**|**What she did**|
|---|---|
|**0:00**|Reads the brief for about 3 minutes. Highlights 3 of the 6 facts.|
|**0:04**|No Google tabs opened. Didn't ping the architect; one fact in the brief linked to their own case study, which answered the question she'd have asked him.|
|**0:05**|Writes the hook. 3 rewrites this time (5 on Friday).|
|**0:19**|Body done, about 170 words. One pass through ChatGPT to tighten.|
|**0:26**|Sends to her manager.|

**26 minutes hands-on, against about 65 on Friday.** What she said at the end: "This is the part I actually hate doing. Don't send me a draft, I'll just delete it."

One person, one session. That's an anecdote, not a pattern yet. The approval wait is still there and the brief doesn't touch it.
